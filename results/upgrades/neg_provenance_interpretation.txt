1. PRIMARY-SOURCE PROVENANCE (25 rows with verbatim quotes, DOIs/URLs: neg_provenance_sources.csv)
  cohn: Cohn, Zuk & Kaplan 2018 (bioRxiv 10.1101/264200) sampled 'a random set of genomic regions (500 bp long), excluding regions with N's, regions less than 15Kb from genes (Zerbino et al., 2018), and H3K27ac bound regions (Villar et al., 2015)'; their code states the negatives 'are not intersected with any gene (+-15000 bases in each direction) and with any peak of H3K27ac'. Positives are human-liver H3K27ac peaks (Villar et al. 2015). No GC, repeat or chromosome matching is described. Genomic Benchmarks did not re-sample: it located the published sequences on GRCh38 (Ensembl 97) by exact match (13,895/14,000 positives, 13,896/14,000 negatives).
  nt_enhancers: the NT card (pinned revision) = Geng et al. 2022: iEnhancer-2L benchmark (742 strong, 742 weak, 1484 non-enhancers) + 6000 synthetic enhancers + 6000 synthetic non-enhancers 'produced with a generative model' (RankGAN); test = the iEnhancer-EL independent set (Liu et al. 2018; 100/100/200), said to be 'constructed based on the same protocol'. iEnhancer-2L (Liu et al. 2016, 10.1093/bioinformatics/btv604) built its set from ChromHMM states of nine ENCODE cell lines; strong enhancers and non-enhancers 'are clearly annotated in all the nine tissues'; 200-bp fragments; CD-HIT 80%; 1484 non-enhancers randomly picked from '5 257 994 non-enhancers'. Which states count as non-enhancer is not stated. ChromHMM state 13 is 'Heterochromatin; low signal' in a nine-mark model that has no H3K9me3 (UCSC track page), so it is described here as unmarked/low-signal chromatin.

7. MECHANISM (stated only as strongly as the data support)
  cohn negatives are gene-distal 500-bp windows (>=15 kb from genes, outside liver H3K27ac peaks; 94.7% >=15 kb from any protein-coding gene vs 46.2% of random windows). The genomic nt_enhancers TRAINING negatives are 200-bp fragments of chromatin that is ChromHMM state 13 (low signal, unmarked) in all nine ENCODE lines (99.8%; 1,484/1,484 by hg19 headers); the TEST negatives (iEnhancer-EL) were cut from single state-13 segments shared by >=2 lines, not the nine-line intersection (64.0% state 13 in all nine). Gene-distal and unmarked DNA is concentrated in AT-rich, L1-rich isochores (L1+L2 71% and 84% vs 60% genome-wide). Backgrounds re-drawn by these rules, with the unstated details inferred post hoc (protein-coding genes; state 13), reproduce the negatives' GC and approximately their TATA rate (cohn pooled GC 0.393 vs 0.393, TATA 0.585 vs 0.574; nt GC 0.375 vs 0.374, TATA 0.352 vs 0.356); the pre-registered cohn rule over-shoots and the pre-registered literal nt rule does not reproduce them. This sampling accounts for 20% (cohn) and 31% (genomic nt; 13.5% over the whole class incl. synthetic) of the positive-negative GC gap and 15% / 30% of the TATA gap; the rest is the enhancer positives' GC-richness (and TATA depletion) relative to random genome.
  Caveats: the cohn gene set and the exact iEnhancer state definition are inferred (protein-coding variant and count reconstruction are post hoc; the count does not separate state 13 from states 13-15); Villar H3K27ac peaks are proxied by the benchmark positives; the iEnhancer-EL test negatives do not follow the nine-line rule (construction inferred from their FASTA headers, not documented); the chr9/chrX/chr2 skew of cohn is unexplained; the gap shares use random genome as the reference.
